29LensExploration · Rev 77 · 5 Nov 2026
Read the evidence through a partner’s framework.

By default Peace Intel answers from the whole literature and takes no institution’s side. A lens lets a reader choose a framework on purpose, for this chat: an organisation’s, so a team reporting to a funder gets answers in the indicators it already uses, or an expert’s way of reading a problem. One control in the composer’s bar opens the lenses as a card in the thread. Each has a switch, a summary under its name and the file Peace Intel actually reads, open to anyone. A lens that is on is named in three places, so nobody reads a framed answer as the neutral one.

What it adds

One control in the bar (.act, the aperture glyph). A card of lenses in the thread, reusing .clarify. A switch, new to the system, at the right of each lens. The lens file in a .dlg, formatted or as Markdown.

How a lens shows it is on

The control fills with ink and names it (Lens · AfP). A line in the thread where it was turned on. A tag over every answer it shaped. All ink: the accent means evidence and action, and a lens is neither.

What it costs

Production: a setting per chat, and the lens file passed to the model with the question. One Markdown file per lens, written or approved by its owner. Logos and names need each organisation’s and each expert’s permission.

Choose a mode, switch on a lens, ask.

Press Normal to choose how far Peace Intel goes (30). Press Lens in the composer. Press a lens’s name to read its summary, and Read the lens file to see the file itself. Switch one or two on, press Done, then send a question.

What should a youth employment programme in northern Nigeria measure to show it reduces support for armed groups?
Peace Intel · 10:48 AM

Across the library the strongest designs measure attitudes and behaviour separately: stated support for violence, and what people do when an armed group recruits. Employment alone moves the first more often than the second, so the studies that showed effects also tracked status, grievance and contact with the state.

The four-way tray asked for a decision before every question and was the widest thing in the bar, wider than the field’s own words. It becomes what the phone already had: one control that names the mode. Pressed, it raises the same card as the lenses, with each mode and what it does, so a reader learns the difference once instead of guessing from a label. A click chooses and closes the card. An answer in any mode but Normal names its mode in its byline. No new CSS: the card is the clarifying questions’ radio tray. What this page has not decided.

One lens or several

Each switch is independent, so two lenses can be on at once. If two frameworks will disagree on a definition, the switches could turn each other off instead.

Where the literature differs

Every lens file asks the answer to close with a note where the wider evidence disagrees. That note is not drawn yet.

Experts’ names

Roger Mac Ginty and Shamil Idriss are real people: their lenses, words and names need their agreement before this is shown outside the team. Dr. Talia Mercer is fictional, labelled so on screen, and must be replaced or removed before any demo.

The modes’ timings

“Takes seconds”, “under a minute” and “a few minutes” are guesses. Nathanael has the real times; if they vary too much to promise, drop them.

One control for both

Mode and lens both shape the answer, so they could share one control and one card (Deep research · AfP). Fewer buttons, but the lens would lose the visibility that makes it a launch feature.

The marks

AfP’s mark is on file; Principles for Peace and Search for Common Ground show a dashed slot until theirs arrive with permission. Experts show their initials.
